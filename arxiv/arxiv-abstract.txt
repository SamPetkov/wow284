The dual degree of a vertex is the average degree of its neighbours. WOW-284 asserts that, for every finite simple connected graph of order at least three and girth at least five, the minimum dual degree is at most minus the least distance eigenvalue. We give exact counterexamples on $38,39,40,42$, and $50$ vertices.

For regular graphs of girth at least five and diameter three, we express the distance matrix as a polynomial in the adjacency matrix. Strict counterexamples then correspond to an interval condition on adjacency eigenvalues. We also prove an order bound without a diameter assumption: if $G$ is finite, simple, connected and $k$-regular, has girth at least five and integer $k\ge4$, and every nonprincipal adjacency eigenvalue $\theta$ satisfies $|\theta+1|\le\sqrt{2k-2}$, then $|V(G)|<(k+2)(k^2+3)/6$. For regular strict counterexamples of diameter three, integer entries of the associated slack matrix give stronger restrictions.

Counterexamples can survive vertex deletion. Every deletion of at most five vertices from the Hoffman--Singleton graph remains a strict counterexample; we exhibit a deletion of six vertices for which strictness fails. If a degree-57 Moore graph of diameter two exists, every deletion of at most $56$ vertices leaves a connected strict counterexample. The arguments combine spectral proofs with exact arithmetic certificates.
