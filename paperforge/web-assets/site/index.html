Counterexamples, Spectral Obstructions, and Deletion Stability for WOW-284

Samuil Petkov · ENS-PSL

A self-contained refutation of a distance-spectral conjecture, together with the shifted adjacency mechanism behind the counterexamples, structural obstructions for regular graphs, and a deletion theory for punctured Moore graphs.

The problem

For a connected graph \(G\), let \(D(G)\) be its distance matrix, let \(\lambda_{\min}(D(G))\) be the least distance eigenvalue, and let \(\delta^*(G)\) be its minimum dual degree. WOW-284 asserts that every connected graph of girth at least five satisfies

\[\delta^*(G)+\lambda_{\min}(D(G))\le 0.\]

The conjecture is natural at the Moore bound, where equality occurs. It fails strictly away from that boundary. The paper gives explicit counterexamples of orders \(38,39,40,42,\) and \(50\). No claim is made that order \(38\) is minimum.

The mechanism

If \(G\) is connected, \(k\)-regular, of girth at least five, and of diameter three, then its distance matrix is an adjacency polynomial:

\[D=3J+(k-3)I-2A-A^2.\]

A nonprincipal adjacency eigenvalue \(\theta\) therefore yields the distance eigenvalue \(k-2-(\theta+1)^2\). Consequently the strict counterexample score is

\[\Phi(G)=2k-2-\max_{\theta\ne k}(\theta+1)^2.\]

WOW-284 becomes an open spectral-window problem centered at \(-1\), rather than an isolated distance-matrix computation.

What the paper adds

On the obstruction side, nonbacktracking trace moments give an exact one-variable linear-programming ceiling with a rigid optimizer. Its integral slack strengthens the order bound, while small principal minors convert the same certificate into local five-cycle and signed-complement restrictions.

These arguments prove that every regular strict counterexample has degree at least six and diameter at most four. In degree six, every regular strict counterexample has order at most \(50\); at the order-\(50\) boundary, the associated signed complement is necessarily disconnected.

On the stability side, deleting vertices from a Moore graph produces an explicit incidence-Gram correction to the distance matrix. Invariant-subspace decompositions give exact spectra for one- and two-vertex punctures, and a uniform perturbation bound separates dual-degree loss from metric damage.

For the Hoffman–Singleton graph, exact orbit calculations sharpen the general estimate to the universal deletion radius proved in the manuscript.

Verification

The analytic proofs are primary. Exact finite computations reconstruct graph distances by integer breadth-first search and certify spectral signs using rational matrix identities, characteristic polynomials, Sturm sequences, and explicitly bounded finite enumeration. The theorem-level decisions do not rely on floating-point eigenvalue comparisons.

The repository records deterministic graph exports, verifier outputs, release manifests, and SHA-256 checksums.

Lean 4.31 verifies the complete \(50\)-vertex graph-level certificate, finite spectral certificates for orders \(38,39,40,\) and \(42\), and the separate all-degree LP optimum and rigidity theorem. The formalization guide states the exact boundary between those kernel-checked results and the remaining analytic arguments.

The public Lean source is proof-complete and contains no project axioms or admitted goals.
