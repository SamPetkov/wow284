# Proof Audit 05: one-vertex and adjacent-edge Moore punctures

**Audited results:** Theorems 3 and 4 in
`VERIFIED_RESEARCH_EXTENSIONS.md`.

**Audit mode:** line-by-line, with the metric reconstruction, invariant-space
splitting, quotient normalisation, multiplicities, least-root comparisons, and
score thresholds treated as separate lemmas.

**Provisional verdict:** `pass_after_proof_expansion`.

The spectrum formulas are correct.  The one-vertex proof compressed the
replacement-path argument and several orthogonality steps.  The adjacent-edge
result was presented only as a proof outline.  This audit supplies complete
proofs without changing any eigenvalue, multiplicity, or threshold.

## 1. Common Moore identities

Let \(M\) be a degree-\(k\) Moore graph of diameter two.  Then

\[
 |V(M)|=k^2+1,
 \qquad
 A(M)^2=(k-1)I-A(M)+J.
\]

Adjacent vertices have no common neighbour and nonadjacent vertices have a
unique common neighbour.  Put

\[
 \Delta=\sqrt{4k-3},
 \qquad
 r=\frac{-1+\Delta}{2},
 \qquad
 s=\frac{-1-\Delta}{2}.
\]

The numbers \(r,s\) are the two nonprincipal adjacency roots.  All multiplicity
formulas below are conditional on the existence of \(M\); their integrality is
then automatic because they are dimensions of explicitly constructed
invariant spaces.

# Part I. Deleting one vertex

## 2. The theorem

Let \(H=M-v\), where \(k\ge2\).  Define

\[
 \rho_\pm=k^2-2\pm
 \sqrt{k(k^3-2k^2+3k-1)},
\]

\[
 m_+=\frac{k(k-2)(\Delta+1)}{2\Delta},
 \qquad
 m_-=\frac{k(k-2)(\Delta-1)}{2\Delta}.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Spec}D(H)={}&\{\rho_+,\rho_-\}\\
 &\cup\{(-2+\sqrt{k})^{(k-1)},
          (-2-\sqrt{k})^{(k-1)}\}\\
 &\cup\left\{
 \left(-\frac{\Delta+3}{2}\right)^{(m_+)},
 \left(\frac{\Delta-3}{2}\right)^{(m_-)}
 \right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \delta^*(H)=k-\frac1k,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(D(H))=-2-\sqrt{k},
\]

and

\[
 \Phi(H)=k-\frac1k-2-\sqrt{k}.
\]

For integer \(k\ge2\), the score is positive exactly when \(k\ge5\).

## 3. Metric reconstruction

Partition the surviving vertices as

\[
 A=N(v),\qquad B=\Gamma_2(v),
\]

with sizes \(k\) and \(k(k-1)\).  Let \(C\) be the \(A\)-by-\(B\) incidence
matrix and let \(B_0\) be the adjacency matrix induced on \(B\).

Every surviving pair keeps its original distance except a pair of distinct
vertices in \(A\).  Such a pair had \(v\) as its unique common neighbour.  Its
new distance is exactly three: for \(a\ne a'\) in \(A\), choose a neighbour
\(b\in B\) of \(a\).  The pair \(b,a'\) is nonadjacent, and its unique common
neighbour cannot be \(v\) or lie in \(A\); it lies in \(B\), giving a path

\[
 a-b-c-a'.
\]

Consequently

\[
 D(H)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 3(J-I)&2J-C\\
 2J-C^{\mathsf T}&2(J-I)-B_0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

This also proves that \(H\) is connected.

## 4. Incidence identities and orthogonal splitting

Block comparison in the Moore identity gives

\[
 CC^{\mathsf T}=(k-1)I,
 \qquad
 CB_0=J-C,
\]

\[
 B_0^2+C^{\mathsf T}C=(k-1)I-B_0+J.
\]

Since \(CC^{\mathsf T}\) is nonsingular, \(C^{\mathsf T}\) is injective.  Also

\[
 C^{\mathsf T}\mathbf1_A=\mathbf1_B.
\]

Hence

\[
 \mathbb R^B=
 \langle\mathbf1_B\rangle
 \perp C^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf1_A^\perp)
 \perp K,
 \qquad K=\ker C,
\]

and

\[
 \dim K=k(k-2).
\]

Combining the \(A\)-space with this decomposition gives three mutually
orthogonal invariant parts of dimensions

\[
 2,\qquad 2(k-1),\qquad k(k-2).
\]

Their sum is \(k^2\).

## 5. Constant quotient

On the normalised cell indicators, the distance matrix has quotient

\[
 Q_1=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 3(k-1)&(2k-1)\sqrt{k-1}\\
 (2k-1)\sqrt{k-1}&2k^2-3k-1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its trace is \(2(k^2-2)\), and its discriminant is

\[
 4k(k^3-2k^2+3k-1).
\]

Thus its eigenvalues are \(\rho_\pm\).

## 6. Incidence modules

For \(z\perp\mathbf1_A\), the two-dimensional space generated by

\[
 (z,0),\qquad(0,C^{\mathsf T}z)
\]

is invariant.  In these coordinates the action is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}-3&-(k-1)\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix},
\]

with characteristic polynomial

\[
 x^2+4x+4-k.
\]

The eigenvalues are \(-2\pm\sqrt{k}\), each with multiplicity \(k-1\).

## 7. Residual kernel

If \(x\in K\), then \(x\perp\mathbf1_B\), the space \(K\) is
\(B_0\)-invariant, and

\[
 B_0^2+B_0-(k-1)I=0
 \quad\text{on }K.
\]

The trace of \(B_0\) on \(K\) is zero: outside \(K\), the constant vector has
eigenvalue \(k-1\), while \(C^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf1_A^\perp)\) has eigenvalue
\(-1\) with multiplicity \(k-1\).  Dimension and trace therefore give
multiplicities \(m_+,m_-\) for \(r,s\).  Since

\[
 D=-2I-B_0
 \quad\text{on }K,
\]

the corresponding distance eigenvalues are

\[
 -\frac{\Delta+3}{2},
 \qquad
 \frac{\Delta-3}{2}.
\]

## 8. Least eigenvalue and score

The residual negative root is strictly greater than \(-2-\sqrt{k}\), because

\[
 \Delta<1+2\sqrt{k}.
\]

The smaller quotient root is also greater.  The symmetric matrix

\[
 Q_1+(2+\sqrt{k})I
\]

has positive first diagonal entry, and with \(t=\sqrt{k}\) its determinant is

\[
 t^2(2t^4+2t^3-3t^2+2)>0
 \qquad(k\ge2).
\]

Therefore

\[
 \lambda_{\min}(D(H))=-2-\sqrt{k}.
\]

The degrees in \(H\) are \(k-1\) on \(A\) and \(k\) on \(B\).  Hence

\[
 d^*(a)=k\quad(a\in A),
 \qquad
 d^*(b)=k-\frac1k\quad(b\in B),
\]

which proves the dual-degree formula.  For \(k\ge5\), the score inequality can
be squared after observing that \(k-2-1/k>0\); it reduces to

\[
 (k^2-2k-1)^2-k^3>0.
\]

At \(k=m+5\), this equals

\[
 m^4+15m^3+77m^2+149m+71>0.
\]

The cases \(k=2,3,4\) are negative by exact substitution.

# Part II. Deleting the endpoints of an edge

## 9. The theorem

Let \(uv\in E(M)\), let \(H=M-\{u,v\}\), and assume \(k\ge3\).  Define

\[
 \sigma_\pm=k^2-3\pm
 \sqrt{k^4-2k^3+3k^2-8k+7},
\]

\[
 a_+=\frac{(k-2)(k+(k-2)\Delta)}{2\Delta},
 \qquad
 a_-=\frac{(k-2)((k-2)\Delta-k)}{2\Delta}.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Spec}D(H)={}&\{\sigma_+,\sigma_-,k-4\}\\
 &\cup\{(-2+\sqrt{k})^{(2k-4)},
          (-2-\sqrt{k})^{(2k-4)}\}\\
 &\cup\left\{
 \left(-\frac{\Delta+3}{2}\right)^{(a_+)},
 \left(\frac{\Delta-3}{2}\right)^{(a_-)}
 \right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \delta^*(H)=k-\frac2k,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(D(H))=-2-\sqrt{k},
\]

and

\[
 \Phi(H)=k-\frac2k-2-\sqrt{k},
\]

which is positive exactly for integer \(k\ge5\).

## 10. Geometry and recomputed distances

Put

\[
 A=N(u)\setminus\{v\},
 \qquad
 B=N(v)\setminus\{u\},
\]

and let \(C\) contain the remaining vertices.  Their sizes are

\[
 k-1,\qquad k-1,\qquad(k-1)^2.
\]

There are no edges inside \(A\), inside \(B\), or between \(A\) and \(B\).
Every vertex of \(A\) and \(B\) has \(k-1\) neighbours in \(C\); every vertex
of \(C\) has one neighbour in each of \(A,B\) and \(k-2\) neighbours in \(C\).

Only pairs inside \(A\) or inside \(B\) lose their unique length-two path.
Their new distance is exactly three.  For distinct \(a,a'\in A\), choose a
neighbour \(c\in C\) of \(a\).  The pair \(c,a'\) is nonadjacent and its unique
common neighbour lies in \(C\), giving

\[
 a-c-z-a'.
\]

The \(B\)-case is symmetric.  If \(F\) is the adjacency matrix of the disjoint
cliques on \(A\) and \(B\), then

\[
 \boxed{D(H)=2(J-I)-A(H)+F.}
\]

## 11. Constant quotient

The row-sum quotient for \(A,B,C\) has the \(A/B\)-antisymmetric eigenvalue
\(k-4\).  On the symmetric constant subspace, the normalised quotient is

\[
 Q_2=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 5k-8&\sqrt{(k-1)(2k-3)(4k-6)}\\
 \sqrt{(k-1)(2k-3)(4k-6)}&2k^2-5k+2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its trace is \(2(k^2-3)\), and its discriminant is

\[
 4(k^4-2k^3+3k^2-8k+7).
\]

Thus its eigenvalues are \(\sigma_\pm\).

## 12. Incidence modules and residual kernel

Let \(R_A,R_B\) be the incidence matrices from \(A,B\) to \(C\), and let
\(T\) be the adjacency matrix on \(C\).  The Moore identity gives

\[
 R_AR_A^{\mathsf T}=R_BR_B^{\mathsf T}=(k-1)I,
 \qquad
 R_AR_B^{\mathsf T}=J,
\]

\[
 R_AT=J-R_A,
 \qquad
 R_BT=J-R_B,
\]

\[
 R_A^{\mathsf T}R_A+R_B^{\mathsf T}R_B+T^2
 =(k-1)I-T+J.
\]

The zero-sum row images of \(R_A\) and \(R_B\) are injective and orthogonal.
Each combines with its cell zero-sum space to give a \(2(k-2)\)-dimensional
invariant module carrying

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}-3&-(k-1)\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Therefore \(-2\pm\sqrt{k}\) each occurs with total multiplicity \(2k-4\).

The residual space

\[
 K=\ker R_A\cap\ker R_B
\]

has dimension \((k-2)^2\).  On \(K\),

\[
 T^2+T-(k-1)I=0,
 \qquad
 D=-2I-T.
\]

The trace of \(T\) on \(K\) is \(k-2\): outside \(K\), the constant direction
has eigenvalue \(k-2\), while the two zero-sum incidence images contribute
\(-2(k-2)\).  Dimension and trace give the multiplicities \(a_+,a_-\).

The complete dimension count is

\[
 3+2(2k-4)+(k-2)^2=k^2-1.
\]

## 13. Least eigenvalue, dual degree, and threshold

The antisymmetric constant eigenvalue \(k-4\) and the residual negative root are
strictly greater than \(-2-\sqrt{k}\).  The smaller eigenvalue of \(Q_2\) is also
greater because

\[
 Q_2+(2+\sqrt{k})I
\]

has positive first diagonal entry and, with \(t=\sqrt{k}\), determinant

\[
 (t-1)(t+1)(2t^4+2t^3-3t^2+2t+6)>0
 \qquad(k\ge3).
\]

Thus the least distance eigenvalue is \(-2-\sqrt{k}\).

The degrees in \(H\) are \(k-1\) on \(A\cup B\) and \(k\) on \(C\).  The
corresponding dual degrees are

\[
 k\quad\text{on }A\cup B,
 \qquad
 k-\frac2k\quad\text{on }C.
\]

For \(k\ge5\), the positive-score inequality reduces to

\[
 (k^2-2k-2)^2-k^3>0.
\]

At \(k=m+5\), this becomes

\[
 m^4+15m^3+75m^2+133m+44>0.
\]

The cases \(k=3,4\) are negative by exact substitution.

## 15. Verdict

Both theorems are valid.  The one-vertex proof needs an explicit metric lemma
and fuller orthogonality bookkeeping.  The adjacent-edge proof outline needs to
be expanded to the complete incidence decomposition above before promotion to a
manuscript theorem.  No mathematical formula changes.
